Farmers Health ሰብሳቢ

Seasonal health for Ethiopian agricultural workers

🌧️

Kiremt (Rainy Season)

Stay Strong in the Field

🌡️

Heat & Sun Protection (ሙቀት)

Drink at least 3–4 liters of water daily during fieldwork. Wear a wide-brimmed hat. Take shade breaks every 2 hours. Signs of heat stroke: dizziness, confusion — seek shade and drink water immediately.

💪

Back & Joint Health

Bend your knees when lifting heavy loads, not your back. Alternate sides when carrying. Stretch your lower back and legs each morning before fieldwork.

🦟

Malaria Prevention (Kiremt season)

Sleep under an insecticide-treated bed net every night. Wear long sleeves at dusk. If you have fever, chills, and headache after working in fields — visit the nearest health post within 24 hours.

🌿

Traditional Farmer Remedies

Koseret tea for stomach pain. Rue (tena adam) for headaches. Ginger root for nausea from pesticide smell. Garlic for infections. Always also visit a health worker for serious symptoms.

🥗

Energy foods for fieldwork

Teff injera provides long-lasting energy. Shiro wot (chickpea stew) is high in protein. Ater (split peas) are iron-rich. Enset (false banana) is great for sustained energy.

💊

Common deficiencies in farm workers

Iron deficiency (anemia) is very common — eat gomen, misir, and teff daily. Vitamin A — eat carrots, sweet potato, mango during harvest. Iodine — use iodized salt always.

🤱

Pregnant farm workers — special care

Avoid heavy lifting and chemical pesticides during pregnancy. Increase protein intake (eggs, ayib, legumes). Take iron + folic acid tablets from your health post.

💧

Hydration plan for harvest days

Morning (before work): 2 glasses water. Every hour in field: 1 glass. Lunch break: 2 glasses. Evening: 2 glasses. Tella/suwa only after work — alcohol dehydrates during fieldwork.

⚠️

Pesticide safety (ፀረ-ተባይ)

Always wear gloves and a mask when applying pesticides. Do NOT eat, drink, or smoke during application. Wash hands and face immediately after. Keep children away from sprayed fields for 24–48 hours.

🐍

Snake & insect bites

Wear closed shoes and thick socks when working. If bitten by a snake: stay calm, immobilize the bitten limb, do NOT cut or suck the wound — go to the nearest health center immediately.

🔪

Injury prevention

Keep tools sharp (dull tools cause more accidents from slipping). Wear protective footwear. If cut: clean with clean water, apply pressure, and visit a health post for stitches if deep.

🌊

Flood season safety

Do not cross flooded rivers or streams. Only drink boiled or treated water after floods. Report unusual illness clusters to your kebele health worker.

Ethiopian farming calendar — health priorities

☀️Tir–Yekatit (Jan–Feb)

🌾 Harvest season. Dust and chaff can irritate lungs — cover nose and mouth. High physical load — rest, eat well. Eye irritation from dust: rinse with clean water.

🌱Megabit–Miazia (Mar–Apr)

🌱 Planting prep. Back strain risk from land preparation. Ensure good nutrition before planting. Water sources may be low — prioritize hydration.

🌦️Ginbot–Sene (May–Jun)

🌦️ Short rains (Belg). Malaria risk rising. Ensure bed nets are intact. Diarrhea from contaminated water — boil all water.

🌧️Hamle–Nehase (Jul–Aug)

🌧️ Kiremt (main rains). Peak malaria season — use treated nets daily. Flooding risk. Respiratory infections from cold and wet. Heavy weeding work — protect back.

🌸Meskerem–Tikimt (Sep–Oct)

🌸 Harvest begins. Allergy season from pollen. Snake activity increases. Tick bites possible during harvest — check skin daily.

❄️Hidar–Tahsas (Nov–Dec)

❄️ Dry cold season. Pneumonia and respiratory infections. Skin cracking from dryness — use shea butter or niter kibbeh.
